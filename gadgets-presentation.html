The Gadgets We Live With

A short tour through ten pieces of technology most of us reach for without thinking twice — from the phone by the pillow to the speaker in the kitchen.

Use the arrows below or your keyboard to move through the slides.

Smartphones

One device replaced a camera, a map, a wallet, a music player and a landline — and then kept adding jobs.

  • A camera good enough to retire the point-and-shoot
  • Banking, tickets and boarding passes in one app
  • The main way most people get online

Laptops

Thin, fast and battery-friendly enough that "working from the laptop" now means anywhere with a chair.

  • Full software, not just simplified apps
  • Video calls, spreadsheets and code on one machine
  • Battery life now measured in a full workday

Tablets

Bigger than a phone, lighter than a laptop — a screen just as happy showing a film as a sketchbook.

  • Reading, drawing and note-taking with a stylus
  • A second screen for video calls or recipes
  • Often a kid's first computer

Wireless Earbuds

No cable to untangle, no cable to catch on a doorknob — just a case that lives in a pocket or bag.

  • Noise cancelling turns a train into a quiet room
  • Quick taps handle calls, music and assistants
  • A charging case that doubles the battery life

Smartwatches & Fitness Trackers

A screen on the wrist that counts steps, tracks sleep and quietly buzzes when a message can wait.

  • Heart rate and sleep tracked without a second thought
  • Notifications filtered before they reach a phone
  • Contactless payments with a tap of the wrist

Smart Speakers & Voice Assistants

Ask a question, set a timer or start a playlist — the room answers without anyone touching a screen.

  • Timers and reminders set hands-free while cooking
  • Music, news and weather on request
  • A hub that ties other smart devices together

Smart Home Devices

Lights, locks and thermostats that respond to a schedule, a voice, or nobody at all.

  • Lights that dim themselves at sunset
  • Doors that unlock for a delivery, not a stranger
  • Thermostats that learn a routine over time

Drones & VR/AR Headsets

Two very different ways to see something from a place your eyes have never been.

  • Aerial footage once reserved for film crews
  • Games and training that put you inside the scene
  • Both still finding their everyday, non-hobby use

Small Devices, Big Habits

None of these existed a generation ago. Now they quietly shape how we work, talk, move and relax — one notification at a time.
